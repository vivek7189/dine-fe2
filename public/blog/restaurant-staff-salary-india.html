How Much to Pay Restaurant Staff in India (2026): Salary by Role and City

October 21, 2026 · 12 min read · Staff & Salaries

Most chefs and cooks in Indian restaurants earn about ₹16,000 to ₹29,000 a month. Waiters earn about ₹16,000 to ₹30,000 in a metro and less in smaller cities. Head chefs and managers sit higher, from roughly ₹26,000 to ₹82,000 depending on where you are and who you compete with for staff. This guide breaks it down by role and city, shows what you pay on top of the salary, and tells you exactly where each number comes from.

Why do salary websites give such different numbers?

Before the tables, one thing to know. We pulled numbers from two big public sources, Indeed India and AmbitionBox, and they don't agree. They are measuring different people.

Indeed's figures come mostly from job posts. Those are often small restaurants and dhabas hiring in a hurry, so the numbers run lower. AmbitionBox figures are reported by employees themselves, and a lot of them work at hotels, cinema chains, caterers and big restaurant groups. Those pay more and offer PF.

So read each range like this. The lower end is roughly what a standalone restaurant pays. The upper end is what a hotel or chain pays. If you run a 30-seat place and a five-star hotel is hiring down the road, you'll feel pressure from the top of the range.

All figures below are monthly gross salary before PF or ESI deductions, rounded. Where a source gave a yearly figure, we divided by 12.

A few things jump out when you look at this as an owner.

Managers in tier-2 cities can cost almost as much as in a metro. The chains that hire them pay similar rates everywhere. If you want a manager with chain experience in Jaipur or Lucknow, budget close to metro pay.

The kitchen helper figures from job posts are low. Indeed shows helpers in Delhi at about ₹11,100 a month, but Delhi's legal minimum for unskilled work has been ₹18,456 a month since April 2025. A job post being common doesn't make it legal. More on that below.

What is the salary of a chef in India?

This is the question we get most, so here it is on its own.

  • Chef or cook, all India: Indeed shows an average of ₹19,868 a month for chefs (596 salaries) and ₹17,546 for cooks (321 salaries). AmbitionBox shows chefs at ₹3.1–3.5 lakh a year, about ₹26,000–29,000 a month (5,100+ salaries).
  • By experience: on AmbitionBox, chefs with 1–3 years report ₹2.8–3.1 lakh a year, and those with 12+ years report ₹5.3–5.8 lakh a year. That is roughly ₹23,000 a month at the start, rising to ₹44,000–48,000 a month.
  • Head chef: Indeed's average is ₹30,068 a month (117 salaries). AmbitionBox shows ₹8.1–9 lakh a year, about ₹67,500–75,000 a month, mostly from hotels and groups.
  • Tandoor chef: AmbitionBox shows ₹2.8–3.1 lakh a year across India, about ₹23,000–26,000 a month. In New Delhi it's ₹2.3–3.4 lakh and in Mumbai ₹2.7–3.5 lakh a year.
  • Commis chef: AmbitionBox shows ₹2.6–2.8 lakh a year (₹21,500–23,500 a month). Indeed shows about ₹1.99 lakh a year (around ₹16,500 a month).

In practice, a good tandoor or Chinese cook who can run a station alone is hard to find in most cities. Expect to pay at the top of the range, or slightly above, for someone with a proven track record. A cook who has only done prep will accept the bottom.

What is the salary of a waiter in India?

Indeed puts the average waiter (it calls them servers) at ₹14,651 a month across India, from 236 salaries. Mumbai is the highest-paying city on that list at ₹19,717. AmbitionBox shows ₹3.2–3.5 lakh a year, about ₹26,700–29,200 a month, but many of those reports are from hotel restaurants.

For a standalone restaurant in a metro, ₹16,000–20,000 is where most waiters land. In Jaipur or Indore, job posts show ₹11,000–12,000, while AmbitionBox shows ₹2–2.8 lakh a year (about ₹16,700–23,300 a month).

Captains, the senior waiters who run a section, earn a little more. Indeed's average is ₹17,509 a month. AmbitionBox shows restaurant captains at ₹2.6–2.9 lakh a year across India.

Waiters also earn tips, and some take home a meaningful amount from them in busy places. But don't count tips as part of the salary when you hire. A waiter compares your fixed pay with the place next door.

What do I pay on top of the salary?

The salary is not the whole cost. Here's what usually gets added.

Food

Almost every restaurant feeds its staff during the shift. Most owners don't track this, but it's real food cost. If 12 people eat two meals a day, that's over 700 meals a month from your kitchen.

Stay

Cooks who move from another state for work often expect a room. Usually it's a shared room near the restaurant. It costs you rent, but it also makes staff easier to find and less likely to leave after a month.

PF (Provident Fund)

PF becomes compulsory once you employ 20 or more people. You and the employee each put in 12% of wages. The compulsory part is calculated on wages up to ₹15,000 a month. So for most restaurant staff your share tops out at ₹1,800 per person per month, and the same amount is deducted from their pay. Contributions above ₹15,000 are optional for both sides.

ESI (health insurance)

In most states ESI applies once you have 10 or more people. It covers staff earning up to ₹21,000 a month. You pay 3.25% of their wages and they pay 0.75%. Staff whose average daily wage is ₹176 or less don't pay their share, but you still pay yours.

A quick example. A waiter on ₹16,000 costs you ₹520 a month in ESI, and ₹120 is deducted from their pay.

Minimum wage

Each state sets its own minimum wages, by skill level, and revises them regularly. Delhi's rates from 1 April 2025 were ₹18,456 a month for unskilled work, ₹20,371 for semi-skilled and ₹22,411 for skilled. Other states are lower or higher. Look up your state labour department's latest notification before you fix pay for helpers and cleaners, because those are the roles most often paid below the floor.

Tips and service charge

Decide early how tips are shared and write it down. Arguments over tips cause more staff fights than salaries do. If you add a service charge to bills, tell guests it's optional and tell your staff how it's split.

Festival bonus and leave

Many owners pay a bonus around Diwali or Eid and give one weekly off. Neither shows up in the salary tables, but staff compare them when choosing where to work.

How much does a full team cost? A worked example

Take a 40-seat restaurant in Indore with a small tandoor and no bar. A place like this usually runs with about 12 people, with the owner acting as manager. Using the lower half of the tier-2 ranges above:

With 12 people, ESI likely applies in Madhya Pradesh. Eleven of them earn under ₹21,000, so your ESI share is about ₹5,000 a month. PF isn't compulsory yet because you're under 20 people. Add staff meals on top.

So the real staff budget for this restaurant is close to ₹1.9 lakh a month before food. If your monthly sales are ₹10 lakh, that's about 19% of sales. How do I keep salaries from eating my profit?

  • Hire for your busiest shift, not your busiest hour. Two part-timers for the dinner rush are often cheaper than one more full-timer.
  • Pay a little more to the people who are hard to replace. Losing a good tandoor cook in peak season costs more than an extra ₹2,000 a month.
  • Keep attendance honest. Paying for days nobody worked adds up fast. A simple attendance record that staff mark themselves fixes most of it.
  • Review pay once a year, at a fixed time. Staff who know when the raise comes are less likely to leave for ₹1,000 more next door.
